## Definition

A time series is an ordered sequence of observations of one indicator for successive points in time or reporting periods.

## Explanation

A time series puts <Term id="observation" /> values of one <Term id="indicator" /> in time order. It shows how the indicator develops and makes the <Term id="trend" /> visible. A single observation says little. A series shows growth, decline, seasonality and gaps.

A common textbook definition describes a time series as "an ordered sequence of values of a variable at equally spaced time intervals". Monitoring data often follows this pattern, for example one value per month. In practice, periods can be missing when a provider does not report. The KB also calls a series with gaps a time series.

All observations in a series use the same <Term id="unit-of-measurement" /> and the same period length. If periods differ, convert them first. See <Term id="period-normalisation" />.

## Example

A service provider reports the number of publications that cite its service for each year from 2019 to 2025. The seven yearly values, in year order, are a time series. A chart of them shows whether citations rise or fall.

## Not to be confused with

- <Term id="observation" />: a single value. The series is the ordered set of them.
- <Term id="cumulative-value" />: a running total calculated from a series.
- <Term id="aggregation" />: combines values into one. A series keeps them separate.
- <Term id="trend" />: the direction of change that you read from a series.
