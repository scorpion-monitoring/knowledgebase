## Definition

Monitoring is determining the status of a system, a process, a product, a service or an activity, carried out at different stages or times.

## Explanation

ISO 9000 defines monitoring as "determining the status of a system, a process, a product, a service, or an activity". Its notes add that this can need checking, supervising or critically observing, and that monitoring is generally carried out at different stages or at different times.

For services, monitoring is periodic and continuous. It includes the periodic technical testing of a service by a monitoring system. It also includes the regular collection of <Term id="indicator" /> values over time, so that a <Term id="trend" /> can be seen.

Monitoring produces a continuous record, such as monthly KPI submissions or periodic tests of an endpoint. <Term id="evaluation" /> interprets that record at specific intervals. In the quality cycle, monitoring covers the observing part of quality control. Comparing the observed value with a goal completes the control loop and needs a <Term id="target" />. Where no target exists, this comparison is deferred to evaluation.

## Example

A data repository tests its public endpoint every five minutes and records whether it answers. It also collects the number of downloads each month. Both are monitoring: the first at a high frequency, the second as a monthly record of use.

## Not to be confused with

- <Term id="evaluation" />: the interpretation of monitoring results against objectives, at intervals.
- <Term id="reporting" />: the communication of monitoring results to others.
- <Term id="measurement" />: the process of determining a single value. Monitoring repeats it over time.
