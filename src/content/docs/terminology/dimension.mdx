## Definition

A dimension is a named group of related criteria in a maturity model that covers one aspect of the capability being assessed.

## Explanation

A <Term id="maturity-model" /> splits a broad capability into dimensions so that each can be assessed and discussed on its own. Each dimension holds one or more <Term id="criterion" /> items. Models use other names for the same part, most often "domain" or "category".

Dimensions let an organisation see its profile. Ratings are compared within a dimension and the results across dimensions show where the gaps are. A dimension normally has no level of its own. Where a model gives one, it is a summary of the criteria beneath it.

The word has other meanings. In statistical data exchange and data cubes, a dimension is a concept, such as reference area or time period, that together with other dimensions identifies a series or an observation. In geometry and physics it is an extent or a measurable quantity. The KB uses the maturity-model meaning only. Where it needs the statistical meaning, it writes "data dimension".

## Example

A model for service management in a research infrastructure has the dimensions "governance", "documentation", "user support" and "usage reporting". The dimension "usage reporting" holds criteria such as "usage statistics are collected" and "statistics are reviewed each year". A service provider reads its ratings per dimension to decide where to improve first.

## Not to be confused with

- A criterion, which is one assessable item inside a dimension.
- A <Term id="maturity-level" />, which is a step on the rating scale and applies to criteria, not to groups.
- A dimension of a data cube, which identifies a data series, for example the reference area.
- A <Term id="thematic-category" />, which groups services by subject.
