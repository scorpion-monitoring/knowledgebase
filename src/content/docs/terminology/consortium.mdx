## Definition

A consortium is a group of independent organisations that cooperate under a shared agreement to reach a common aim, such as running a research infrastructure.

## Explanation

Members keep their own legal identity. They agree on rules for governance, shared services and reporting. Research communities also call such a group a "network", a "federation" or a set of "nodes". The KB uses "consortium" and lists "network" as an alias.

Usage varies. In funding programmes a consortium is the set of partners in one funded project. In research infrastructure policy it can be the long-lived group behind an infrastructure. The KB follows the second usage and says "project consortium" when it means the first.

A consortium has members, and each member can be a <Term id="service-provider" />. Together they can offer the services of a <Term id="research-infrastructure" />.

This page gives a working definition. No single standard defines the term for research infrastructures.

## Example

Twenty institutes form a national network for life-science data. Each institute operates some services. The network's management office collects usage numbers from all members to report to the funder.

## Not to be confused with

- A <Term id="research-infrastructure" />, which is the set of facilities and services. A consortium is one way to organise it.
- A <Term id="service-provider" />, which is one member organisation that delivers a <Term id="service" />.
- A European Research Infrastructure Consortium (ERIC), which is a specific legal form defined in EU law.
