## Definition

The mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) is the mean of the absolute differences between actual and forecast values, each divided by the absolute actual value.

## Explanation

For actual values aₜ and forecasts fₜ, MAPE is (1/n) × Σ |aₜ − fₜ| / |aₜ|, often multiplied by 100. It measures how far a forecast lies from what happened, in percent of the actual value. Lower is better. The measure is scale-free, so you can compare forecasts for series of different sizes.

MAPE has known weaknesses. It is undefined when an actual value is zero and unstable when actual values are close to zero. It also treats over-forecasts and under-forecasts unequally. The forecasting literature discusses these problems and recommends alternatives such as scaled errors.

In common usage, the abbreviation "MAPE" means this forecast error. Some sources use similar words for the spread of a series around its own mean. The KB keeps the two apart. It uses mean absolute percentage error only for forecast accuracy, and the <Term id="mean-absolute-percentage-deviation" /> for dispersion within one series.

MAPE needs two inputs: a forecast and the actuals it predicted. A single <Term id="time-series" /> alone does not give a MAPE.

## Example

A service provider forecasts 100, 120 and 80 support requests for three months. The actual numbers are 110, 100 and 80. The percentage errors are 10 / 110 ≈ 9.1 %, 20 / 100 = 20 % and 0 %. The MAPE is about 9.7 %.

## Not to be confused with

- <Term id="mean-absolute-percentage-deviation" />: the spread of a series around its own mean, with no forecast involved.
- <Term id="percentage-change" />: the change between two observed values, not an error against a forecast.
- <Term id="target" />: a value to reach, not a prediction to be scored.
