## Definition

A leading indicator signals results that are still to come, and a lagging indicator confirms results that have already happened.

## Explanation

The two words describe timing, not importance. A lagging indicator reports an outcome, such as the number of publications that acknowledge a service. It is simple to verify but arrives late, so it cannot change the outcome. A leading indicator reports something that tends to come before the outcome, such as the number of new users who start using a service. It arrives early but only predicts.

Kaplan and Norton describe a balance between outcome measures, which they call lagging indicators, and the performance drivers that lead to them, which they call leading indicators. The <Term id="results-chain" /> gives the same idea a different form: earlier links in the chain lead, later links lag.

Usage varies. Some authors treat the terms as properties of a pair of indicators, and the same indicator can lead one result and lag another. Economic statistics use "leading indicator" in a narrower sense for a <Term id="time-series" /> that moves before the business cycle. The KB uses the broader management sense.

A <Term id="trend" /> in a leading indicator is a reason to look at the lagging indicators it should affect. It is not proof of a change.

## Example

A research infrastructure tracks the number of training courses it runs each year. This is a leading indicator for the number of new users the next year. It tracks the number of publications that use its services as a lagging indicator. A rise in course numbers is an early sign. A rise in publications, years later, is the confirmation.

## Not to be confused with

- <Term id="key-performance-indicator" />: an indicator that is critical to success. A key indicator can be leading or lagging.
- trend: the direction of a series of values. A trend can be seen in either kind of indicator.
- The economic "leading indicator" of the business cycle, which is a narrower usage.
