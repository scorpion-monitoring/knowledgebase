## Definition

A key performance indicator (KPI) is an indicator, quantified by a metric, that focuses on an aspect of performance critical to an organisation's current and future success.

## Explanation

Not all indicators are equally relevant to managing a service. The indicators that an organisation deems most important are its KPIs. The general KPI literature, citing Parmenter, describes KPIs as "a set of measures focusing on those aspects of [organisational] performance that are the most critical for the current and future success of the [organisation]" (spelling adjusted).

Applied to research infrastructures, KPIs monitor the development, performance and user acceptance of services and establish control mechanisms within joint projects.

The difference between an <Term id="indicator" /> and a KPI is one of selection and purpose, not of kind. In the German Network for Bioinformatics Infrastructure (de.NBI), for example, the KPIs of each service category are a subset of possible indicators that the network agreed on after extensive discussion. Service providers are expected to report them.

A KPI needs a specification beyond its name. The literature commonly lists these features: an identifier, a name and a description; a calculation rule; a <Term id="value-type" /> and a <Term id="unit-of-measurement" />; a frequency of calculation; a <Term id="target" /> value or range; and the data source from which the KPI is computed. The target is the counterpart of the service level in a service level agreement. It is the goal against which quality control compares the measured value.

The literature also reports that there is no consensus on the elements of a KPI definition. The KB follows the specification above and says so where a page depends on it.

## Example

A federation of service providers agrees that every web application reports "unique visitors per month" and "service availability per month". These two indicators are the KPIs of the web-application service category. A provider may track further indicators for its own use, but those are not KPIs of the federation.

## Not to be confused with

- <Term id="indicator" />: any evidence for a component of an impact pathway. A KPI is a selected indicator.
- <Term id="metric" />: how an indicator is quantified.
- <Term id="target" />: the value a KPI is meant to reach. A KPI can exist without one.
- <Term id="headline-indicator" />: one indicator chosen to summarise a set.
