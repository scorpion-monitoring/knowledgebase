## Definition

A quantitative indicator is directly measurable as a number, while a qualitative indicator describes a quality that is not directly measurable, such as user satisfaction.

## Explanation

The literature on key performance indicator management calls the same split *hard* and *soft*. Hard indicators are directly measurable, for example the number of users. Soft indicators are not, for example user satisfaction. The KB uses "quantitative" and "qualitative" and treats "hard" and "soft" as aliases.

A qualitative indicator is still evidence. It can be expressed through a survey score, a coded interview or a narrative. The ELIXIR Impact Toolkit lists narratives, case studies and lists of examples as supplementary evidence next to indicators.

The split is not the same as the position of an <Term id="indicator" /> on the <Term id="results-chain" />. Indicators near the impact end of the chain tend to be more qualitative, but the classification by position depends on the role of the indicator, not on its data type.

## Example

"Number of registered users" is quantitative. "Satisfaction of trainees with a course" is qualitative when it is reported as a rating on a scale of agreement, although the ratings can be counted afterwards.

## Not to be confused with

- Quantitative and qualitative *research methods*, which describe how data are collected and analysed.
- <Term id="indicator-classes" />: a grouping by what the indicator observes about a service.
