## Definition

A research infrastructure is a facility, or a set of facilities, that provides resources and services to research communities so that they can conduct research and foster innovation.

## Explanation

The European Commission uses this wording and lists typical components: major equipment or sets of instruments, knowledge-related facilities such as collections, archives or scientific data infrastructures, computing systems and communication networks. The legal definition is in Article 2 of Regulation (EU) 2021/695 (Horizon Europe).

A research infrastructure can sit at one site, be virtual, or be distributed over several sites and organisations. It can offer physical access, remote access, or both.

Policy documents describe research infrastructures in slightly different words. The KB follows the Commission wording above, because it is the basis for European funding and reporting.

A research infrastructure usually offers many <Term id="service" /> items. Each one has a <Term id="service-provider" />. A distributed research infrastructure is often run by a <Term id="consortium" /> of organisations.

## Example

A national life-science data infrastructure offers a sequence-analysis platform, a cloud compute service and a training programme. Several host institutes run these services. The infrastructure's management counts users, publications and service availability for each service, so it can report to its funders (see <Term id="monitoring" />).

## Not to be confused with

- A <Term id="service-provider" />, which is the organisation that runs one or more services, not the whole infrastructure.
- A <Term id="consortium" />, which is a group of organisations. A research infrastructure may be run by one, but the two are different things.
- A European Research Infrastructure Consortium (ERIC), which is a specific legal form for establishing and operating a research infrastructure of European interest.
