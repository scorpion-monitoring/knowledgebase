## Definition

A permission is the approval to perform one specific kind of action, such as reading or editing a record, on a resource.

## Explanation

A permission is the smallest unit of <Term id="authorisation" />. It names an action, and often a type of resource. Permissions are usually written so that each one allows exactly one thing.

Permissions are rarely given to people one by one. They are grouped into a <Term id="role" />, and people receive the role.

Vocabulary varies. Operating systems usually say "privilege". Role-based access control standards say "permission". Some glossaries treat "authorisation" and "permission" as synonyms. The KB uses "permission" for a single allowed action and "authorisation" for the wider decision.

## Example

A usage-statistics form is protected by three permissions: to view the figures, to enter new figures and to approve them. A provider contact holds the first two. A coordinator holds all three. The permission to approve is not given to the person who enters the figures, so that two people check each value.

## Not to be confused with

- <Term id="role" />: a named set of permissions.
- <Term id="authorisation" />: the decision or approval in general.
- File-system permissions: one implementation of the idea, in operating systems.
