## Definition

Evaluation is the determination of the suitability, adequacy or effectiveness of an object, such as a service or a portfolio, to achieve established objectives.

## Explanation

For research infrastructures the object of an evaluation is a service, a service portfolio or the infrastructure as a whole. Evaluation draws on evidence produced by <Term id="monitoring" />. In particular, an <Term id="impact-assessment" /> starts from an impact pathway that states which activities are intended and what evidence is collected for each component.

**Where usage differs.** ISO 9000 has no entry for "evaluation". The matching entry is "review", defined as the "determination of the suitability, adequacy or effectiveness of an object to achieve established objectives", with management review and the review of corrective action as examples. The KB uses "evaluation" because the research-infrastructure literature does, for example for impact evaluation. The definition above is the ISO definition of review applied to that usage.

Monitoring is continuous. Evaluation happens at specific intervals, such as an annual service review in a network or the periodic review of a set of core data resources. Where monitoring has no <Term id="target" />, the comparison with a goal is made in the evaluation.

## Example

A network of service providers collects monthly usage figures all year. Each autumn a panel reviews the figures against the aims of each service and decides whether to continue, change or retire it. The collection is monitoring. The panel's review is evaluation.

## Not to be confused with

- <Term id="monitoring" />: the continuous observing that feeds an evaluation.
- <Term id="impact-assessment" />: an evaluation that focuses on long-term effects.
- Evaluation in the sense of assessing a person or a proposal, which is not meant here.
