## Definition

A self-assessment is a rating of an organisation's own capability, carried out by the people responsible for it, against the criteria of a model.

## Explanation

In a self-assessment the assessed party and the assessor are the same. The organisation reads each <Term id="criterion" /> of a <Term id="maturity-model" />, gathers its evidence and chooses a <Term id="maturity-level" />. This is cheap, it builds shared understanding, and it fits regular repetition.

The weakness is bias. People tend to rate their own work generously, and different organisations read the same criterion differently. Clear level descriptions and a requirement to name evidence reduce this. They do not remove it.

Other assessment forms involve a second party. A peer review is done by colleagues from comparable organisations. An external audit is done by a body that is independent of the organisation. Auditing standards call an audit by or on behalf of the organisation itself an internal or first-party audit. The KB uses "self-assessment" for the rating of a model and keeps "audit" for a formal, evidence-based process with defined independence.

Results of a self-assessment are best used for improvement and comparison over time. They are weaker as proof of compliance, because nobody has checked them.

## Example

Each service provider in a research infrastructure completes a yearly questionnaire based on a maturity model. The provider selects a level for each criterion and gives a link to its evidence, such as a published user guide. The infrastructure reads the answers as stated and may then ask a sample of providers for a peer review.

## Not to be confused with

- An external audit, which an independent body performs against requirements.
- A peer review, which colleagues from other organisations carry out.
- <Term id="reporting" /> of indicator values, which supplies measured data instead of a judgement of capability.
- An <Term id="impact-assessment" />, which estimates effects of an activity on others.
