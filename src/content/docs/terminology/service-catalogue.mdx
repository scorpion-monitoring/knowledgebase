## Definition

A service catalogue is a user-facing list of the live services that a provider or infrastructure offers, with the information users need to choose and request them.

## Explanation

IT service management standards and the FitSM family require providers to keep a service catalogue. It shows what users can request, for example a description, access conditions and contact.

A catalogue is usually a subset of a larger service portfolio. The portfolio is an internal list that also includes services in preparation and services that are retired (see <Term id="lifecycle-stage" />). The catalogue shows only what is live.

People often use "catalogue" and "registry" for the same thing. The KB keeps them apart. A catalogue serves users who choose a <Term id="service" />. A <Term id="service-registry" /> is a record of services with metadata for discovery and reporting, and it can hold more than live services.

The spelling "catalog" is common in American English. The KB writes "catalogue".

## Example

An infrastructure publishes a web page with all its live services. Each entry gives a name, a short description, the provider and how to request access. A service still in development does not appear there, but it is on the internal portfolio.

## Not to be confused with

- A <Term id="service-registry" />, which records services for discovery and reporting and may include non-live ones.
- A service portfolio, which is the internal, wider list of all services in every <Term id="lifecycle-stage" />.
- A <Term id="research-infrastructure" />, which is the organisation that offers the services.
