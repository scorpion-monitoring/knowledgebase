## Definition

A service provider is an organisation, or a part of one, that manages and delivers one or more services to users or customers.

## Explanation

The provider is the party that is accountable for a <Term id="service" />: it runs it, supports it and answers for its quality. The provider can be a whole institute, a department, a project team or a company.

In a <Term id="research-infrastructure" />, the providers are often member institutes that each operate some of the services. One service can have one provider or several.

The term has a second meaning in IT service management, where an "external service provider" can mean a supplier that delivers a supporting service to the main provider. The KB uses the first meaning only: the organisation that delivers the service to its users.

The same organisation can act as provider for some services and as user of others.

## Example

An institute runs a data-archive service and a workflow-execution service. It is the service provider for both. Its team reports yearly usage, availability and publications for each service to the research infrastructure's management.

## Not to be confused with

- A <Term id="research-infrastructure" />, which can include many providers and many services.
- A <Term id="consortium" />, which is a group of organisations. A consortium may hold several service providers, or be one.
- A user or customer, who receives the service.
