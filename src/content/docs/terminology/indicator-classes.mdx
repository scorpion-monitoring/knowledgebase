## Definition

Operational indicators describe the technical state of a service, usage indicators describe how it is used, and scientific impact indicators describe its effects beyond direct use.

## Explanation

**This is a working definition of one author, not a community standard.** It comes from the doctoral thesis of the author of this knowledge base (unpublished). The thesis derives it from three sources: the baseline monitoring criteria for operational indicators of the EOSC-Synergy project, the key performance indicator (KPI) catalogue of the German Network for Bioinformatics Infrastructure (de.NBI) for usage and citation-based indicators, and the RI-PATHS typology for impact.

The three classes are:

- *Operational indicators* describe the technical state of a service. Examples are the availability of public endpoints, response time and error rates.
- *Usage indicators* describe how a service is used. Examples are visits, unique users, downloads and executions.
- *Scientific impact indicators* describe the effects of a service beyond its direct use. Examples are citations of the service and its contribution to research outputs.

The classification is a second axis next to the <Term id="results-chain" />. The results chain classifies an <Term id="indicator" /> by its place on the path from resources to effects. This classification groups by what is observed. The two are related. Operational and usage indicators usually document activities and outputs, which an organisation controls. Scientific impact indicators document outcomes and lie at the lower end of the sphere of interest, where effects depend on other actors.

The <Term id="thematic-category" /> groups indicators by subject and is a third, separate grouping.

## Example

A database provider reports three indicators. The availability of its web interface is operational. The number of unique visitors per month is a usage indicator. The number of publications that cite the database is a scientific impact indicator.

## Not to be confused with

- Activity, outcome and impact indicators of RI-PATHS, which classify by position on the results chain. See <Term id="results-chain" />.
- <Term id="thematic-category" />: the grouping by bibliographic, usage, technical and satisfaction themes.
- <Term id="quantitative-and-qualitative-indicators" />: the grouping by data type.
