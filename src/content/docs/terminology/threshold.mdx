## Definition

A threshold is a limit value of an indicator that, when crossed, signals a problem or triggers a defined decision or action.

## Explanation

A threshold divides the range of an <Term id="indicator" /> into acceptable and not acceptable. Crossing it can raise an alert, start a review or change a status. A threshold can be an upper limit, such as a maximum response time, or a lower limit, such as a minimum availability.

Thresholds and targets differ in purpose. A <Term id="target" /> is what you want to achieve. A threshold is the point at which you must act. A service can meet its minimum threshold and still miss its target. Thresholds are often less strict than targets. Some indicators have several thresholds, for example one for a warning and one for a critical state.

Usage varies between communities. In monitoring and operations practice, a threshold is a value at which an alert or action starts. In results-based management, the term is less common, and "target" and "benchmark" cover most uses. The KB follows the monitoring usage.

A threshold compares with a single <Term id="observation" />. It does not describe the change since the <Term id="baseline" />.

## Example

A research infrastructure sets the threshold for the indicator "Service availability" at 95 per cent per month. The target is 99 per cent. A provider with 97 per cent misses the target but crosses no threshold. A provider with 93 per cent crosses the threshold, and the infrastructure asks for a recovery plan.

## Not to be confused with

- <Term id="target" />: the aim, not the point that triggers action.
- <Term id="baseline" />: the starting value, not a limit.
- <Term id="observation" />: a recorded value that you compare with the threshold.
