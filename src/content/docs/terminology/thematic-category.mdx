## Definition

A thematic category is a subject group, such as bibliographic, usage, technical or satisfaction, to which an indicator is assigned.

## Explanation

Thematic categories help readers find and compare <Term id="indicator" /> values by subject. A typical set of categories is:

- **Bibliographic:** indicators from publications and citations.
- **Usage:** indicators of how many people use a service and how often.
- **Technical:** indicators of how a service performs, such as availability.
- **Satisfaction:** indicators of how users rate a service.

These four are the KB's working set. A community can use other categories. The categories belong to the <Term id="indicator-framework" /> and are open to change.

Other classifications exist and answer different questions. <Term id="indicator-classes" /> divides indicators into operational, usage and scientific impact indicators. The RI-PATHS logical model separates what an infrastructure does (activities and outputs) from its outcomes and its impacts. These classifications describe where an indicator sits on the way from activity to effect. A thematic category describes the subject. An indicator has one thematic category in a framework, and it can also belong to a class.

## Example

A framework lists "publications acknowledging the service" as bibliographic, "number of user requests" as usage, "availability" as technical and "user rating" as satisfaction. The annual summary shows the indicators grouped by these four headings.

## Not to be confused with

- indicator classes: operational, usage and scientific impact indicators. A different classification, though the word "usage" appears in both.
- <Term id="necessity-level" />: the expectation to report, not the subject.
- indicator framework: the whole set, in which categories are one feature.
