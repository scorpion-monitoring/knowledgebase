## Definition

Reporting is the structured delivery of indicator values and their context to a defined audience, at agreed times and in an agreed format.

## Explanation

<Term id="monitoring" /> collects data on <Term id="indicator" /> values. Reporting passes those values on. The audience can be a funder, a governing body, a partner organisation or the public. The audience, the schedule and the format are agreed before the first report.

A report is more than a table of numbers. It states the <Term id="reporting-period" /> that the values cover, the unit and the way each value was obtained, and any known limits of the data. Without this context, a reader cannot compare values from different sources.

Reporting is one-directional delivery. Judging what the values mean is the task of <Term id="evaluation" />. A report can feed an evaluation, but it does not replace it.

Usage varies: some communities call any internal data entry "reporting", others reserve the word for delivery to an external audience. The KB uses the broader sense: delivery to any defined audience, internal or external.

## Example

A research infrastructure asks each of its service providers to report, once a month, the number of user requests, the number of publications that acknowledge the service and the share of time the service was available. Each provider sends the values in the same template. The infrastructure's office checks the submissions for completeness and passes a combined summary to its governing board each quarter.

## Not to be confused with

- monitoring: the continuing collection and tracking of data. Reporting is the delivery of the results.
- evaluation: the judgement of worth or merit. Reporting supplies evidence for it.
- <Term id="measurement" />: the act of obtaining one value. A report holds many measurements.
