## Definition

Impact assessment is the systematic study of the longer-term effects that an activity, service or infrastructure has on research, society and the economy.

## Explanation

<Term id="monitoring" /> tracks what an infrastructure does and produces, usually every month or year. Impact assessment asks what changed because of it. The effects appear later, often years later, and other factors also shape them.

The question is one of attribution. An assessment tries to separate changes that the infrastructure caused from changes that would have happened anyway. The OECD DAC glossary describes this aim for impact evaluation. This is why impact assessment needs methods such as comparison groups, case studies or expert judgement, and not only <Term id="indicator" /> values.

Impact assessment is a form of <Term id="evaluation" />. The <Term id="results-chain" /> shows where it sits: it looks at the far end of the chain, beyond activities and outputs.

Usage varies. In evaluation practice, "impact" means long-term effects. In everyday speech, and in some scientometric work, it can mean any measured influence, such as citations. The KB follows the evaluation usage.

## Example

A research infrastructure offers sequencing services. Each year it reports the number of projects served and the number of resulting publications. Every five years it commissions an assessment. The assessment interviews users and funders and traces how the services changed the research of its users, the training of staff and the work of industry partners.

## Not to be confused with

- monitoring: routine tracking of indicators. It supplies data to an assessment but does not judge effects.
- evaluation: the wider activity. Impact assessment is one kind of evaluation.
- <Term id="citation-count" />: one indicator of influence. It is evidence for an assessment, not an assessment.
