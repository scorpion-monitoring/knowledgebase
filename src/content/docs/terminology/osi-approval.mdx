## Definition

OSI approval is the status the Open Source Initiative grants to a licence after its review process finds that the licence complies with the Open Source Definition.

## Explanation

The Open Source Initiative (OSI) is a non-profit organisation that maintains the Open Source Definition. A licence submitted to its review process is checked against the criteria of that definition. If it passes, OSI lists it as approved.

Approval is a property of the licence, not of a piece of software. Software is open source in this sense when it is released under an approved licence.

Other bodies keep their own lists, and a licence may be accepted by one and not by another. The KB uses OSI approval as its test for an <Term id="open-source-licence" />, because the status is public and can be checked. A licence list that records the status, such as the one behind the <Term id="spdx-licence-identifier" />, lets tools check it automatically.

## Example

A funder asks a research infrastructure to report how many of its software services are open source. The infrastructure counts the services whose licence identifier appears in the list of OSI-approved licences. Services with a custom licence, or one that has not been reviewed, are reported as "not OSI-approved" and are checked by hand.

## Not to be confused with

- <Term id="open-source-licence" />: the general concept. A licence can follow the principles and still lack OSI approval.
- Certification of the software or of its security: approval covers only the licence.
- Free software approval by other organisations: a separate list, which overlaps strongly with OSI's.
