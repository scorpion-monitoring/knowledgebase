## Definition

A bibliometric indicator is a quantitative measure derived from publication and citation data, used to describe the output or influence of research.

## Explanation

Examples are the number of publications, the <Term id="citation-count" />, and measures that adjust counts for field or year. Indicators for journals and for individual authors also exist. A bibliometric indicator is a specific type of <Term id="indicator" />.

Indicators have limits. Their results depend on the data source, the time window, the field and the counting method. They describe publication behaviour, not the quality of research or of a service.

The research evaluation community has set out principles for use. The Leiden Manifesto offers ten principles to guide research evaluation, and warns that evaluation is increasingly driven by data and not by expert judgement. The San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA) recommends not using journal-based metrics, such as journal impact factors, as a surrogate measure of the quality of individual articles or in hiring, promotion or funding decisions.

The KB uses bibliometric indicators as context for monitoring, next to other indicators, and not as a ranking of services.

## Example

An infrastructure reports for each service the number of publications that acknowledge it and their total <Term id="citation" /> links, with the data source and date. It does not combine them into a single score.

## Not to be confused with

- A <Term id="citation-count" />, which is one simple bibliometric indicator.
- A key performance indicator (KPI), which measures progress towards an organisational goal. See <Term id="key-performance-indicator" />.
- An alternative metric (altmetric), which is based on social media or web attention and not on citations.
